# When to See a Doctor

## 1. Why Is Medical Advice Important?

Irregular periods, acne, excess hair growth, and other symptoms can have several possible causes.

These symptoms may occur in people with PCOS, but they do not automatically mean someone has PCOS.

A healthcare professional can review symptoms, medical history, and appropriate tests to help identify the cause.

## 2. When Should Someone Arrange a Routine Appointment?

Consider arranging an appointment with a healthcare professional if:

- Your periods have become irregular or your usual pattern has changed.
- You have missed three periods in a row.
- Your periods last longer than seven days.
- You have bleeding between periods.
- You have irregular periods together with acne, increased facial hair, or unexplained weight changes.
- You are concerned about possible PCOS.
- Your symptoms affect your everyday activities or emotional well-being.

Do not ignore persistent symptoms simply because they are common.

## 3. When Should Someone Seek Prompt Medical Assessment?

Seek prompt medical advice if:

- You notice rapid or sudden changes in hair growth or other physical symptoms.
- You have severe or worsening pelvic pain.
- You have heavy bleeding that is unusual for you.
- You may be pregnant and experience vaginal bleeding or abdominal or pelvic pain.

A healthcare professional can determine how urgently you need to be assessed.

## 4. When Is Emergency Care Needed?

Seek emergency medical care if you experience:

- Very heavy bleeding accompanied by fainting, severe dizziness, shortness of breath, or chest pain.
- Sudden, severe abdominal or pelvic pain, particularly if pregnancy is possible.
- Severe weakness or fainting with possible pregnancy and abdominal pain or bleeding.

Do not wait for an online symptom guide or chatbot to respond before seeking emergency care.

In India, call 112 for emergency assistance when needed, or go to the nearest emergency department.

## 5. Which Healthcare Professional Can Help?

Depending on the symptoms, a person may start with a general physician or consult a gynaecologist.

An endocrinologist may be involved when hormonal or metabolic problems require specialist assessment.

The appropriate professional depends on the person's symptoms, medical history, and local healthcare options.

## 6. What Information Should Someone Prepare?

Before an appointment, it may help to record:

- The dates when periods begin and end.
- Whether bleeding is light, moderate, or heavy.
- Any pelvic pain or other symptoms.
- Changes in acne or hair growth.
- Medicines and supplements currently being taken.
- Questions or concerns they want to discuss.

This information may help the healthcare professional understand the situation.

## 7. Important Safety Note for OvaCare

OvaCare provides general educational information. It cannot diagnose PCOS or determine with certainty how urgent a person's condition is.

When a user reports possible emergency warning signs, OvaCare should prioritise clear advice to seek urgent or emergency care rather than continuing a routine PCOS explanation.

If symptoms are unclear or concerning, the app should encourage professional assessment rather than provide false reassurance.
