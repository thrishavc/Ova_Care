# Tests Doctors May Suggest for PCOS

## 1. Why Might a Doctor Recommend Tests?

PCOS stands for Polycystic Ovary Syndrome. A healthcare professional may recommend tests to understand a person's symptoms, assess hormone levels, or check for other conditions that can cause similar symptoms.

Not everyone needs the same tests. The choice depends on symptoms, age, medical history, and clinical assessment.

## 2. Medical History and Physical Examination

A doctor may ask about:
- Menstrual cycle patterns.
- Acne or increased facial and body hair.
- Medicines and supplements.
- Relevant medical history.
- Family history of diabetes or hormonal conditions.

The doctor may also check blood pressure and other relevant health measurements.

## 3. Blood Tests for Hormones

Depending on the symptoms, a doctor may request blood tests to assess certain hormone levels.

These tests can help evaluate signs of higher androgen levels and investigate other possible causes of irregular periods or similar symptoms.

No single hormone blood test confirms PCOS in every person.

## 4. Tests to Check Blood Sugar

PCOS is associated with an increased risk of problems with blood glucose regulation.

A doctor may suggest a glucose test to assess blood sugar status.

Possible tests include:
- **Oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT):** Measures how the body handles glucose after a glucose drink.
- **Fasting blood glucose:** Measures blood glucose after a period without food.
- **HbA1c:** Estimates average blood glucose over approximately the previous two to three months.

The 2023 international PCOS guideline identifies the OGTT as the most accurate test for assessing glucose status in people with PCOS. Other tests may be considered when an OGTT is not practical.

The appropriate test should be decided by a healthcare professional.

## 5. Cholesterol and Blood Pressure Checks

A doctor may check cholesterol and other blood fats, as well as blood pressure, to assess metabolic and cardiovascular health.

These checks are important because PCOS is associated with certain metabolic health risks.

## 6. Thyroid and Other Tests

A doctor may request additional tests to investigate conditions that can cause symptoms similar to PCOS.

For example, thyroid testing may be appropriate when thyroid problems are a possible explanation for menstrual changes.

Other tests depend on the person's symptoms and medical history.

## 7. Is an Ultrasound Always Necessary?

No. An ultrasound is not necessary for every person being assessed for PCOS.

In adults, diagnosis may sometimes be made using menstrual or ovulation problems and signs of higher androgen levels, after other causes have been excluded.

In some circumstances, a doctor may use an ultrasound to examine the ovaries. The 2023 international guideline also recognises anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) as an alternative to ultrasound for assessing polycystic ovarian morphology in adults, when appropriate.

Ultrasound and AMH are not recommended for diagnosing PCOS in adolescents because they are not sufficiently specific in this age group.

Having multiple small follicles on an ultrasound does not automatically mean someone has PCOS.

## 8. Can OvaCare Tell Me Which Tests I Need?

No. OvaCare cannot select tests for an individual or interpret their results as a diagnosis.

A healthcare professional should decide which tests are appropriate. Do not order tests or begin treatment solely because an app suggests them.

## 9. Important Safety Note

Tests are only one part of a medical assessment. A diagnosis may require consideration of symptoms, medical history, examination findings, and other possible causes.

OvaCare provides general educational information and does not replace a medical consultation.
