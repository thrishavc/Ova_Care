# Common Myths About PCOS

## Myth 1: Irregular Periods Always Mean PCOS

**Fact:** Irregular periods can have many causes, including stress, pregnancy, thyroid problems, and PCOS. A healthcare professional can help determine the cause.

## Myth 2: Everyone With PCOS Has Ovarian Cysts

**Fact:** Despite its name, visible ovarian cysts are not required for a PCOS diagnosis. Some people can have PCOS without polycystic ovaries on an ultrasound.

## Myth 3: Only People With Higher Body Weight Get PCOS

**Fact:** PCOS can affect people with different body types and body weights. Weight alone cannot confirm or rule out PCOS.

## Myth 4: Everyone With PCOS Has the Same Symptoms

**Fact:** Symptoms vary. Some people experience irregular periods, acne, or increased hair growth, while others have different symptoms.

## Myth 5: PCOS Always Causes Infertility

**Fact:** PCOS can affect ovulation and make pregnancy more difficult for some people. However, many people with PCOS can become pregnant, sometimes with medical support.

## Myth 6: PCOS Can Be Confirmed From Symptoms Alone

**Fact:** Symptoms help guide a medical assessment, but they are not enough on their own to confirm PCOS. A healthcare professional considers appropriate diagnostic criteria and other possible causes.

## Myth 7: Everyone With PCOS Must Lose Weight

**Fact:** People with PCOS have different health needs. Healthy habits can support health even when body weight does not change. Weight management advice should be individualised and respectful.

## Myth 8: PCOS Has One Guaranteed Cure

**Fact:** There is currently no single cure for PCOS. However, treatment and lifestyle support can help manage symptoms and reduce certain long-term health risks. Treatment should be tailored to the individual.

## Myth 9: Everyone With PCOS Needs an Ultrasound

**Fact:** An ultrasound is not always necessary. The tests used depend on the person's age, symptoms, medical history, and clinical assessment.

## Myth 10: PCOS Is Someone's Fault

**Fact:** PCOS is a complex hormonal and metabolic condition. It is not a personal failure. People experiencing symptoms deserve respectful care without shame or blame.

## Important Safety Note

OvaCare provides general educational information. It cannot diagnose PCOS, recommend individual treatment, or replace a qualified healthcare professional.

If you have persistent or concerning symptoms, seek medical advice.
