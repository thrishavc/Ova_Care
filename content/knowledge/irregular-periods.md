# Irregular Periods

## 1. What Are Irregular Periods?

Irregular periods happen when the timing, length, or amount of menstrual bleeding changes from the usual pattern.

A menstrual cycle is counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next period.

For many adults, menstrual cycles usually occur about every 21 to 35 days. Cycles can differ during the first years after periods begin and as menopause approaches.

## 2. What Can Cause Irregular Periods?

Irregular periods can happen for several reasons, including:

- **PCOS:** A hormonal condition that can affect ovulation and the timing of periods.
- **Stress and anxiety:** Stress can affect the menstrual cycle.
- **Weight changes:** Significant weight loss or gain may affect periods.
- **Thyroid problems:** Changes in thyroid hormone levels can affect menstruation.
- **Exercise:** Excessive exercise may contribute to irregular periods.
- **Pregnancy:** A missed period can be an early sign of pregnancy.
- **Hormonal contraception:** Some contraceptive methods can change bleeding patterns.
- **Life stages:** Periods may be irregular during puberty and the transition to menopause.

These are possible causes, not a diagnosis. A healthcare professional can help identify the cause.

## 3. Is an Irregular Period a Sign of PCOS?

Irregular periods can occur in people with PCOS, but they do not automatically mean someone has PCOS.

Other conditions and life changes can also affect periods. A healthcare professional may ask about symptoms, medical history, and perform appropriate tests before reaching a conclusion.

OvaCare cannot confirm PCOS from a person's symptoms alone.

## 4. When Should Someone See a Doctor?

Consider arranging a medical appointment if:

- Your periods have become irregular or your usual pattern has changed.
- You have missed three periods in a row.
- Your period lasts longer than seven days.
- You have bleeding between periods.
- You have irregular periods along with increased facial hair, acne, or unexplained weight changes.
- You are concerned about your menstrual cycle or are having difficulty becoming pregnant.

A doctor can assess your symptoms and decide whether tests or treatment are needed.

## 5. When Is Urgent Medical Care Needed?

Seek urgent medical attention if you have very heavy bleeding, especially if you are soaking through a pad every hour and feel faint, dizzy, short of breath, or have chest pain.

If you have a missed period or possible pregnancy together with severe abdominal or pelvic pain or bleeding, seek urgent medical assessment.

Do not wait for OvaCare to assess an emergency.

## 6. What Can Someone Do Before a Medical Appointment?

Keeping a simple period diary can help you describe your symptoms.

Record:

- The first day of each period.
- How many days the bleeding lasts.
- Whether the bleeding is light, moderate, or heavy.
- Any pain or other symptoms.
- Any noticeable changes in your usual cycle.

Share this information with a healthcare professional if you seek care.

## 7. Important Safety Note

OvaCare provides general educational information. It does not diagnose PCOS or other conditions, prescribe medicines, or replace a healthcare professional.

Do not start, stop, or change medication based only on information provided by OvaCare.

Seek professional medical advice for persistent or concerning symptoms and urgent care for emergency warning signs.
