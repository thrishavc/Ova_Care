# Weight and Insulin in PCOS

## 1. What Is Insulin?

Insulin is a hormone made by the pancreas. It helps glucose (sugar) move from the blood into the body's cells, where it can be used for energy.

## 2. What Is Insulin Resistance?

Insulin resistance happens when the body's cells do not respond to insulin as effectively as they should.

The body may then need to produce more insulin to help control blood glucose levels.

Insulin resistance is associated with PCOS and may contribute to metabolic health problems in some people with this condition.

## 3. How Are PCOS, Insulin, and Weight Related?

PCOS can be associated with insulin resistance and a higher risk of developing type 2 diabetes.

Some people with PCOS experience weight gain or have difficulty managing their weight. However, PCOS affects people differently, and not everyone with PCOS has a higher body weight.

Weight alone cannot confirm or rule out PCOS.

## 4. What Healthy Habits May Help?

Healthy habits can support general and metabolic health, whether or not a person loses weight.

Helpful habits may include:

- Eating a balanced variety of foods.
- Including vegetables, fruits, whole grains, and suitable protein sources.
- Doing regular physical activity that suits individual abilities and circumstances.
- Getting adequate sleep.
- Attending recommended medical appointments and health checks.

There is no single diet or exercise plan that is best for everyone with PCOS. Avoid extreme diets and unnecessary food restrictions.

## 5. Does Everyone With PCOS Need to Lose Weight?

No. Weight loss is not appropriate or necessary for everyone with PCOS.

Healthy lifestyle habits can provide benefits even when body weight does not change. Advice about weight management should be individualised and respectful.

OvaCare must never shame users about their weight or assume that weight loss is their goal.

## 6. Should Someone Take Medicine for Insulin Resistance?

OvaCare must not recommend starting medication or supplements to treat insulin resistance.

A healthcare professional can assess a person's health, determine whether testing is appropriate, and discuss treatment options if needed.

Do not take diabetes medicines or other prescription medicines without appropriate medical guidance.

## 7. What Health Checks Might a Doctor Discuss?

Depending on the person's circumstances, a healthcare professional may assess blood glucose levels and other metabolic risk factors, such as cholesterol and blood pressure.

The appropriate checks and their timing depend on individual health needs and clinical guidance.

A person should not assume they have insulin resistance based on weight or symptoms alone.

## 8. Important Safety Note

OvaCare provides general educational information. It cannot diagnose PCOS, insulin resistance, or diabetes and cannot prescribe treatment.

For personal concerns, consult a qualified healthcare professional.
